## 0. The answer in short

**Keep the owner's scoring idea; replace the "every business on the map" enumeration with a bounded, multi-source funnel.** The owner's idea has two halves. The *evaluation* half — read each candidate's hours, days closed, website, menu, rating, review count and the reviews that touch his preferences, score everything, recommend the top — is right and becomes the scoring stage of the method. The *enumeration* half — "literally go business to business on Google Maps" — is the wrong way to produce candidates, for five reasons that are each sufficient on their own:

1. **Terms.** The Maps Platform terms forbid "bulk download [of] … places information" and "copy and save business names, addresses, or user reviews" (General Terms §3.2.3(a)). A city-wide enumeration is a bulk download whether or not we store it. A bounded candidate set produced by targeted queries is ordinary use of the API.
2. **The API cannot do what the sweep assumes.** A place returns at most **5 reviews, chosen by Google ("sorted by relevance"), with no sort or filter parameter**, so "reviews relevant to my preferences" cannot be fetched per place; Nearby Search returns **at most 20 places per call with no pagination**, ranked by **popularity** by default — in a dense tile the obscure places, the ones we want, are exactly the ones dropped.
3. **Cost scales with density, and density is highest where trips go.** A full sweep costs tens of dollars in a compact city and hundreds in a dense capital (§5), against a method that stays inside Google's monthly free caps.
4. **Time.** A `/plan` is one routine run. Reading and judging thousands of websites and menus takes hours; reading forty takes minutes.
5. **"High rating, few reviews" is also the fingerprint of a fake-review-boosted new business.** A sweep that ranks on those two numbers alone will recommend some of them. Gems need corroboration from outside Google.

**Recommended method — the Gem Funnel (§4):** interview (adds a *gem appetite* setting) → taste-specific queries in English and the local language with a rating floor → three candidate streams (Google taste queries · local voices from editorial and community web research · quiet neighbourhoods found with Google's area counts) → dedupe by place id → free screening on the fields the search already returned → a gem score (preference fit · Bayesian-shrunk quality · obscurity relative to peers · local-ness from sources · practicality) → an evidence pass on the top ~40 (own website and menu, the 5 API reviews and Google's review summary where it exists, fake-review check) → the §5.9 shortlist with 💎 marks and a one-line "why it's a gem", plus a "Gems not chosen" Later list. About $4 per `/plan` at list price, $0 inside the free caps (§5). Nothing persists but place ids, our own notes and the owner's taps.

**What it needs (§6):** three interview questions, Nearby Search and the Places Aggregate API in the Maps kit, a `gems/` scoring module in the engine, a local-source plan in the research skill, 💎 handling in the shortlist, and the owner enabling one more Google API at Phase 7. **Decisions for the owner (§7):** gem appetite default, when to enable the Aggregate API, whether the routine may read the API reviews, third parties later, manual seeds.

**What "hidden gem" means operationally** (the score in §4 encodes exactly this): a place that is (a) *good* — rated well by enough people to trust it; (b) *under-exposed* — far fewer reviews than its peers of the same kind in the same city; (c) *locals' territory* — named by local-language or local-editorial sources, not by mass-tourism lists; (d) *his* — fits the profile; (e) *practical* — open on a trip day, reachable, not a two-hour queue. It is **not** a place that is merely new, merely obscure, or merely cheap.

**Verdict.** B + C + D generate and rank the candidates; A's evaluation idea becomes stages 2–4; E enriches the finalists where Google offers it; F is a free manual seed; G and H are out for now. The combination is stronger than any one stream because each covers the others' blind spot: B reaches places no guide has written up, C reaches places whose Google listing is thin (few reviews, no English), D keeps both honest with numbers, and the evidence pass checks the winners against the place's own website and against fake-review fingerprints.

## 4. The recommended method: the Gem Funnel

Runs inside `trip-research` as the candidate-generation and scoring part of §5.9 step 3. Budgets are per `/plan` round; a *More options* or *More gems* round reruns stages 1–5 with the already-shown places excluded.

**Stage 0 — Interview (three questions added to §5.1).** `gem_appetite` (1–5: "stick to the famous" … "I'd rather eat where the neighbourhood eats"); `off_track_minutes` (how far beyond the day's plan a gem may pull him, by transit or on foot); `rough_edges` (which of cash-only, no English menu, queues, no reservations, standing room he tolerates). All three are closed questions, so they apply directly to the profile. Default when unanswered: appetite 3, 25 minutes, tolerates cash-only and no English.

**Stage 1 — Candidate streams (budgeted).**
- *Stream 1, taste queries (Google).* The skill derives 20–40 queries from the profile × destination: cuisines, named dishes, activity types, each crossed with the trip's neighbourhoods and phrased both in English and in the destination language (fact: local listings are reviewed in the local language). Text Search **Enterprise**, `minRating` 4.3 (4.5 at appetite ≥ 4), `pageSize` 20, one page by default and a second only when the first was all high-count places, `locationRestriction` on the trip's bounding area, `strictTypeFiltering` where the query is a type. Budget 40–80 calls → 800–1,600 place-results with name, types, rating, review count, price level, hours, website, `businessStatus`, lat/lng.
- *Stream 2, local voices (web).* A research-kit run with a **per-destination source plan**: two editorial lists, two local-language sources, one or two community threads, each chosen by the skill from a short table of source kinds per country (Japan: Tabelog + a local magazine; most of Europe: the city paper's food section + a local-language blog; the US: the city's Eater and Infatuation lists + the city subreddit). Budget ≤ 25 searches / 40 fetches. Every place a source names is resolved to a place id with a free IDs-only Text Search (name + area), and the ledger ref, the source's language and whether it is editorial or community are kept with the candidate (`local_mentions`). Typical yield 50–150 places. Page text stays data (the kit's scanner and quarantine rules apply unchanged).
- *Stream 3, quiet neighbourhoods (Google, area-level).* For each of the trip's categories (restaurant, cafe, bar, museum, park, …) and each neighbourhood cell the skill lays over the trip area (circles of 400–800 m around our own geocoded anchors — stations, squares, the lodging — never polygon tests on Google coordinates), one Places Aggregate `computeInsights` count with `ratingFilter` ≥ 4.5 and operating status operational. Budget 20–40 calls. Cells with a high count of well-rated places but far from the tourist anchors and from the places already chosen get one Nearby Search **Enterprise** each, `rankPreference` DISTANCE, radius 300–600 m, `includedTypes` for the category. Budget 10–20 calls → 200–400 results. Where the Aggregate count for a cell is ≤ 100, its place ids can be taken directly instead.
- *Stream 4, owner seeds (manual, free).* Names the owner pastes into the chat — from the Maps app's Local Gems list, a friend, a newsletter — resolved like stream 2 and tagged `owner_seed` (they skip the obscurity test but not the quality or practicality tests).

Dedupe by place id. Typical pool after dedupe: 600–1,500 unique places, each with search-tier fields and the stream(s) that found it.

**Stage 2 — Free screening (no calls).** Drop: `businessStatus` not operational; chains (the same display name appears three or more times in the pool, or matches a short chain list); rating below the floor; fewer than 15 ratings unless two or more local mentions (too new to trust); closed on every trip date; beyond `off_track_minutes` from every anchor by the trip's modes (straight-line estimate at this stage); types the profile avoids. Keep 200–400.

**Stage 3 — Gem score (0–100) for every survivor, from fields already in hand.**
- *Quality Q*: Bayesian-shrunk rating `(n·r + m·μ) / (n + m)` with `μ` = the pool's mean rating for the place's category (default 4.2 when a category has fewer than 20 members) and `m` = 30; mapped linearly from 4.0 → 0 to 4.9 → 1.
- *Obscurity O*: 1 − the percentile rank of `userRatingCount` among the pool's places of the same category (the Aggregate counts calibrate how big the city is); bucketed so that 40–400 ratings in a large city (15–150 in a small one) score highest, and anything above 2,000 scores 0. `owner_seed` places get O = 0.5 by convention.
- *Local-ness L*: +0.5 per independent local-language source that names the place (cap 1.0); +0.3 for an English-language local editorial source; +0.2 for a community thread; −0.5 when the place sits in the top ten of a mass-tourism list the research found. Clamp 0–1.
- *Fit F*: the model's judgement 0–1 of the profile against types, price level, the sources' descriptions and, after stage 4, the reviews, summaries and the place's own site. Before stage 4 it is a cheap estimate from types, price and source text.
- *Practicality P*: 1 when open at a usable time on at least one trip day and within the day's travel budget; reduced by known queue or reservation friction the owner does not tolerate (`rough_edges`).
- `gem = 100 × (0.35·F + 0.25·Q + 0.20·O + 0.15·L + 0.05·P)`; `gem_appetite` moves up to 0.10 of weight from Q and F to O and L (appetite 5) or the reverse (appetite 1). A place is marked 💎 when `O ≥ 0.6` **and** `L ≥ 0.3` **and** `Q ≥ 0.6`; everything else is an ordinary candidate ranked by the same score, so one scorer serves the mainstream shortlist too. The weights are starting values; Phase 8 tunes them from the owner's ✅ 🔖 ❌ taps.

**Stage 4 — Evidence pass on the top ~40** (the top 24 by score plus up to 16 💎 by gem score). For each: one Place Details **Enterprise + Atmosphere** call (the ≤ 5 reviews, `reviewSummary` and `editorialSummary` where present, full hours, website, price range) and, through the research kit, one fetch of the place's own website or menu page (its own site is not Google content; it is `--official` for the hours claim). The model then sets F properly and flags: **unproven** (all five reviews within the last 60 days and under 50 ratings and no local mention → not a 💎, shown with the label), **tourist-oriented** (English-only menu with tourist pricing, "popular with visitors" wording, a mass-tourism listing), **closed-day conflict**. Hours and closed days become research-kit claims under the two-source rule exactly as today.

**Stage 5 — Shortlist and Later list (§5.9 step 3, decision 15 unchanged).** Still 8 activities + 6 food per round. A floor of 💎 slots by appetite: appetite 1 → none; 2 → 1 + 1; 3 → 2 + 2 (default); 4 → 3 + 2; 5 → 4 + 3. Each 💎 line carries, besides the existing why-you line, minutes, area, Maps link and confidence label, a one-sentence **"why it's a gem"** built from numbers and sources, never from review text ("4.7 from 180 ratings where peers average 1,900; named by two local-language guides; no English menu"). Buttons as today plus *More gems* (one routine fire: stage 1 streams 2–3 again with the shown places excluded). Scored places that were not shown land in a Later list "Gems not chosen" with the reason, so `/later` can promote them. **Superseded by R3 (Phase 6): `gem_line` is words-only — no rating value, count or peer median — see `helpers/status/WP-6b.md`.**

**Compliance, built in.** Persist only place ids, our own notes, scores we computed and the owner's taps; search-tier snapshots are build-scoped and purged at run end (the Maps kit's existing purge). Reviews and AI summaries are read in-run only and displayed, if at all, with author attribution and the Google Maps link (the brochure already does this for one credited review per card) — never paraphrased as Tour Guide's own words. The shortlist is Tour Guide's ranking of its own pool, labelled as such, with the attribution block as today; it never claims to be Google's search result. Area questions go to the Aggregate API; distances use our own anchors. Nothing from Google is used to train or tune a model.

**The sweep, for comparison** (*estimates with stated assumptions, not quotes*). In a dense capital — where this owner's trips tend to go — the bill rules it out as well.

## 8. Confidence

- **Confirmed today on the official pages:** every price and free cap in fact 5; the 20-result and no-pagination limits of Nearby Search; the 60-result cap and filters of Text Search; "a maximum of 5 reviews … sorted by relevance"; the AI-summary regions; the §3.2.3 clauses and §14 of the Service Specific Terms; the Aggregate API's filters and 100-id rule.
- **Inference, to be checked in WP-2g before anything depends on it:** that Text Search relevance matches query words against review text (if not, stream 1 still works on names, types and descriptions, and stream 2 carries the "locals" signal alone); that the Places Aggregate API is enabled on a free-trial project without an upgrade; the obscurity buckets and the score weights (starting values for Phase 8); that chain detection by repeated display names is good enough (a short known-chain list is the fallback).
