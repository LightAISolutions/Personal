# WP-14b — decisions (Scout's ranking, tuned)

Brief: `helpers/prompts/TG-PHASE-14.md` "WP-14b" (item 7). Contract as built: `helpers/decisions/TG-SCOUT.md` §4 and §10.
Every number lives in `helpers/packs/tour-guide/scout/scout-weights.mjs`.

1. **Quality anchor: food 4.2, activities 4.3, anything else `QUALITY.mu_default` (4.2)** — the brief's defaults. `rankScout` always resolves the group to food or activities, so the fallback is for `muFor` callers only. Old boards keep their stored scores; nothing is recomputed.
2. **Chain penalty 8, crowd penalty 4, both off the rounded score, floored at 0.** The brief's numbers. Penalties come after rounding (score = round(weighted parts) − penalties) so a card's five bars still reproduce the pre-penalty number exactly and the penalty is a whole number of points the owner can read.
3. **Crowd threshold = gems `CROWD_MAGNET_MIN_COUNT` (2 000), re-exported as `CROWD_MIN_COUNT`.** The planner reads the gem screen's `crowd_magnet` flag (planner-input.mjs) rather than computing its own, and that flag needs ≥ 2 000 ratings (plus a top-decile or mass-tourism rank). Scout uses the count alone: the top-decile half is a property of the pool, which would bring back the "means something different on every board" fault change 1 removes. A chain past the threshold pays both penalties.
4. **No new label or field for the penalties.** `chain` is already a label; a crowd magnet has no label in C14, and the brief adds none. The why line is unchanged.
5. **Rescue: relevance ≥ 0.7 (inclusive) or veg `verified`.** Only the `unproven` screen is lifted; low rating, off topic, diet and distance still apply. A rescued place is labelled `new` by the engine: kept with fewer than 5 ratings and no local mention, which can only happen through the rescue. A judgment's own `new` label still passes through as before.
6. **Food kind from the place first, then the query.** `foodKind(record, what)` reads the place's name and primary type (underscores as spaces, so `tea_house` reads "tea house"; plurals with -s / -es); the stricter of the two wins (meal > café / market > drink), so "Sake and Sushi" typed `bar` stays strict. Only when neither names a kind does the query decide ("coffee" → drink, "dinner" → meal); with nothing at all the kind is meal (strict). The full `types` list is not read: Google tags many cafés `restaurant` too, and the primary type is the one Google stands behind.
7. **Word lists as the brief gives them**, plus `cafe` for `café` (diacritics are folded) and nothing else: no "kitchen", no "food court". Drinks: bar, sake, wine, beer, cocktail, coffee, tea, tea house, matcha, kissaten, juice. Cafés and sweets: cafe, sweets, dessert, wagashi, bakery, ice cream, parfait. Markets: market, food hall. Meals: ramen, udon, soba, kaiseki, izakaya, lunch, dinner, sushi, tempura, okonomiyaki, curry, teishoku, restaurant.
8. **Diets.** "Likely" for drinks needs a vegetarian or vegan diet (`/\bvegetarian\b/`, `/\bvegan\b/`); for cafés, sweets and markets a vegetarian one. Any other diet (gluten-free, say) keeps the strict screen. The brief's "the vegan rule already drops desserts and bakeries" is honoured by not extending likely to cafés for vegans.
9. **Google's explicit `servesVegetarianFood: false` keeps the strict screen.** The lists fill silence, they do not overrule a place that says it serves nothing vegetarian. This also keeps the old diet test's "Google says no" case meaningful.
10. **`veg: "none"` reads as `"no"`.** The brief's test names "none"; the engine's enum is verified / likely / no / unknown. Accepting the alias costs nothing and closes a gap where "none" silently counted as unknown (and would now have passed a bar as likely).
11. **Likely is the `veg_likely` label only when there is a diet.** Without a diet there is no screen and no likely label from the word lists (as Google's flag already behaved).
12. **Unjudged fit 0.3; `not_judged` whenever the judgment has no numeric `fit`**, even when it has relevance or veg — C14 defines the label as "the judgment step gave no fit for this place". It is last in the label order, so on a crowded card (> 8 labels) it is the first to go.
13. **`parts.local` is an integer 0–100 in the payload**, like the other four parts (C14's "0–1" is the engine's part). The schema and the core accept it optionally, so old payloads validate. The payload emits the keys in the card's order: topic, quality, fit, local, reach.
14. **Board bars**: the grid flows by column (`grid-auto-flow:column`) so five bars and an old card's four both fill the row. **App bars**: inline styles inside the Scout screen only (no stylesheet change), using the page's `--line` and `--accent` tokens; values are clamped integers, so a hostile part cannot inject style.
15. **Chat line**: `🆕` after the name for `new`, and "· not judged" for `not_judged`, next to the existing 💎 🌱 🔁 marks.
16. **One estimator: `estimateReach(from, to, { fromStations?, toStations? })`.** The planner's `walkMinutes` up to 20 minutes, else `railEstimate`. 20, not the engine's old 30: TG-SCOUT §8 records that the private driver turns a walk over 20 minutes into a train estimate, and one estimator means one limit. Without station lists each end stands for its own station (a one-minute walk at each end, then the ride with its wait and transfer); railEstimate may still answer "walk" when that is quicker. With station lists but no usable pair, the end points stand in. `reachFor` takes the quickest estimate over all anchors (before: the nearest anchor by straight line).
17. **Modes**: a train estimate is reported as `TRANSIT` (the payload's enum has no TRAIN), a walk as `WALK`; always `estimated: true`.
