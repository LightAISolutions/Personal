## 2. The two specs that now sell MV solutions — ride-through and flicker

Jacky's framing is not just right — it understates how fast the ground moved. In the three weeks before this report, voltage ride-through went from a preference to **binding ERCOT law**, and the flicker problem acquired its first proposed numerical limit. This section is the technical narrative the US sales motion should be built on.

### 2.1 Ride-through: from incident reports to a rule with teeth

**The record that forced the rule.** On 10 July 2024, a single failed lightning arrestor near Fairfax, VA produced six successive sub-100ms voltage sags — and ~1,500 MW of load, "exclusively data center-type load," disconnected itself; ~1,260 MW stayed off **for hours**, because UPS disturbance-counting schemes (three sags in a minute → stay on backup until *manual* reconnection) latched the fleet off the grid [NERC-0125]. On 22 July 2026 it happened again, bigger: a transmission fault near Ashburn dropped **over 3 GW** of data-center load off PJM at once [PJM-0726]. ERCOT's own ledger: **26 events of >100 MW indirect load loss** from computational facilities between January 2023 and September 2025 — facilities shedding 17–95% of consumption on sags as shallow as 0.844 p.u., some taking hours to return [NERC-0126]. ERCOT's VP of System Operations put the systemic risk plainly: sudden frequency changes from mass load trips "can cause other loads and generators to trip offline, potentially resulting in cascading outages" [ERCOT-BOD].

**The rule.** NOGRR282 + NPRR1308 — approved by the PUCT on 9 July 2026, **effective 1 August 2026** — create the "Large Computational Load" (LCL) class: any site ≥75 MW aggregate peak with ≥50% computational load. LCLs must ride through defined voltage/frequency envelopes (e.g., sags to 0.50–0.80 p.u. for ≥0.5 s; to 0.20–0.50 p.u. for ≥0.25 s), **return to ≥90% of pre-disturbance consumption within 2 seconds** of voltage recovery, cap disturbance current draw at 125% of normal, use filtered measurements with time delays — and **disturbance-counting trip schemes are explicitly banned**. Failures trigger a root-cause/corrective-plan regime, and ERCOT may order a non-compliant LCL disconnected [ERCOT-1308, ERCOT-SPWG]. Two nuances that matter commercially: the rule is **not retroactive** (loads approved before 14 Nov 2025 are exempt — so the retrofit sale there is voluntary, but every new build is in scope), and facilities energizing **after 1 Jan 2028** face a stricter keep-consuming tier during 0.5–0.8 p.u. sags [ERCOT-SPWG]. Texas SB6 adds the political layer: mandatory remote-disconnection equipment for new ≥75 MW loads and ERCOT curtailment authority [SB6].

**And it is going national.** NERC escalated from a Level 2 alert (Sept 2025) to a rare **Level 3 Essential Action alert (4 May 2026)** with seven required actions on computational loads [NERC-ALERT]; on **16 July 2026 FERC ordered NERC to file mandatory reliability standards for computational loads** — Phase I due 31 December 2026 [FERC-RD26]; MISO's large-load initiative explicitly lists ride-through, ramping and stability criteria [MISO-LL]. A vendor selling ride-through performance in ERCOT today is selling next year's national requirement.

**Why conventional chains trip (the physics, all from NERC's incident forensics [NERC-0125, NERC-0126]):** static UPS transfer to battery and count disturbances; DRUPS return to grid only manually; constant-power PSUs draw *more* current as voltage falls (0.8 p.u. voltage → 1.25 p.u. current; 150–200% spikes observed), tripping overcurrent and neutral-overcurrent protection; cooling VFDs with undervoltage thresholds observed as high as 0.96 p.u. drop out in 50 ms sags and force thermal shutdowns.

**Why Zhonhen's architecture rides through by construction (company-stated specs; the mapping is analysis).** In the HVDC/Panama chain the battery strings hang **directly on the DC output busbar** — there is no transfer event, no counting logic, and no static switch to latch: a grid sag is buffered at the rectifier and the IT load never sees it [ZH-DOSSIER, DECK]. Centralized MV rectification puts one controlled current path where a conventional facility has hundreds of distributed breakers, letting the 125%-current rule be engineered at a single point; the deck's "no power surge / −60% server-PSU failure" claim is the same property stated as reliability [DECK]. **The commercial translation: make the NOGRR282 envelope the pilot's acceptance test.** Demonstrating a compliant sag ride-through on a container-level test converts a Chinese field record into an ERCOT compliance artifact — the single most persuasive object a US buyer can be handed. (Precision for the call: quote ERCOT's record as "26 events over 100 MW" and attribute the 1.5 GW and 3 GW events to Northern Virginia/PJM — getting the geography right signals command of the primary documents.)

### 2.2 Flicker: the load's own misbehavior, about to be regulated

**The record.** Microsoft, OpenAI and NVIDIA jointly published the defining paper (Aug 2025): synchronized training swings aggregate power "by tens or hundreds of megawatts," with the swing spectrum concentrated at **0.2–3 Hz — overlapping the torsional and inter-area oscillation bands of grid turbine-generators**, which the authors warn "can cause physical damage to the power grid infrastructure" [ARXIV-PS]. Meta disclosed tens-of-MW instantaneous fluctuations training Llama 3 [LLAMA]. NERC's task force published high-speed recordings of a 50 MW block of an AI data center ramping at **1.9 p.u. per second** [NERC-LLTF]. ERCOT has briefed since July 2025 on LCL power variation exciting "torsional modes of synchronous generators" [ERCOT-LLWG].

**The rules forming.** ERCOT has proposed a **10 MW peak-to-peak limit on LCL active-power variation within any rolling 5-second window** (0.1–55 Hz evaluation band, PMU monitoring) — still a proposal in the stakeholder process as of this writing, with the revision request planned from Q2 2026 [ERCOT-LLWG]; NPRR1234 already embeds subsynchronous-oscillation requirements for large loads (effective Dec 2025) [ERCOT-1234]; NERC's May 2026 draft guideline recommends transmission owners adopt **IEEE 1453 flicker limits** into large-load interconnection requirements [NERC-GUIDE].

**The mitigation market — and where it validates Zhonhen.** NVIDIA's own GB300 answer is power smoothing: capped ramps, **65 joules of capacitor storage per GPU on the power shelf**, and a "GPU burn" taper — claiming 30% peak-demand reduction [NV-GB300]. A supercapacitor-shelf market has formed around the same idea (Eaton's 420 kW XLHV module, Delta's Power Capacitance Shelf, Skeleton's GrapheneGPU shelves, Musashi/Flex CESS racks) [SUPERCAP]; xAI buffers Colossus with 168+ Tesla Megapacks and Aligned deployed a 31 MW/62 MWh BESS explicitly as an interconnection asset [BESS-BUF]. **The reading (analysis): the industry has converged on Zhonhen's architectural premise — storage coupled to the power chain at multiple depths — and is assembling it piecewise from retrofit parts. Zhonhen ships it as one designed system: capacitor and battery units inside the 1 MW sidecar (150 A/μs load response), BBU shelves in the rack chain, battery compartments in the MVR container, campus BESS in front (with CATL, Section 4.1) — all on one DC bus [DECK].** Off-grid, the argument is stronger still: an islanded gas-fired campus has no grid inertia, so the 0.2–3 Hz swing hits the engines directly — the buffered DC backbone is generator protection (Section 5.2, Crusoe).

### 2.3 What this means commercially

The sequencing follows: sell in ERCOT first (where the rule is live and the targets in Section 5 are building), make compliance the acceptance test, and carry the FERC timeline as the reason the rest of the country follows by 2027.

## 3. The field — where Zhonhen sits among SST and MV-solution providers

**The frame that organizes everything: there are two competing paths from medium voltage to 800VDC, and NVIDIA has now put dates on both.** Path one is the **transformer-rectifier unit (TRU)** — a line-frequency transformer plus centralized rectification, the architecture class Zhonhen productized as Panama/MVR in 2019. Path two is the **solid-state transformer (SST)** — power electronics replacing the transformer entirely. NVIDIA's August 2026 blog lays out the productization ladder it is driving with Google and Microsoft through OCP: side-mounted **power racks in 2H 2026**, centralized **row power centers in 2027**, and data-hall **DC power blocks — the step that converts MV AC directly to 800VDC, including via SST — as a 2029 target** [NV-0826, SR-0826]. An LVDC SST specification reached only v0.3 in July 2026 [NV-0826].

**The strategic reading (analysis):** the Western ecosystem's own roadmap reaches Zhonhen's core capability *last*. One-stage MV-to-DC conversion is the 2029 rung of NVIDIA's ladder — and it is the rung Zhonhen has been shipping commercially since the Alibaba co-development, with a claimed 15-year, 10GW+ delivered field record behind it [DECK]. That defines the window: **2026–2029, before Western SSTs productize, is when a field-proven TRU vendor is selling something nobody in the West can ship at scale.** Schneider Electric's own published architecture note says exactly this out loud: at scale, MV converts directly to 800VDC "either via a solid-state transformer (SST) or a transformer rectifier unit (TRU)" — and Schneider has announced no SST product [SE-800V].

### 5.3 The common entry motion (analysis)

1. **Sell the schedule first** — factory-built MV-to-DC skids against a 30–40-month domestic transformer queue and scarce site electricians; the tariff conversation loses to the calendar conversation in 2026 procurement [ZH-BRIEF].
2. **Sell power quality as the spec** — the ERCOT ride-through requirement and GPU flicker (Section 2) are the two criteria where Zhonhen's architecture wins on physics, not on price. Make the pilot's acceptance test a ride-through and load-step demonstration.
3. **De-risk visibly** — pilot pricing, embedded engineering, extended warranty backstops (CATL balance sheet, Section 4.1), air-gapped controls as a spec commitment, and a named US service partner (the ABB relationship today; the Schneider structure if it matures).
4. **One lighthouse beats breadth** — a single accepted ERCOT pilot at IREN or Crusoe converts the China field record into an American reference; until then every deal is missionary work. Sequence accordingly.
